The core innovation of Dex-One2Many lies in its use of sequential scene graphs derived from the human video. This abstraction serves two critical functions: it enables the generation of diverse reset states for RL training, allowing for exploration beyond the exact configurations seen in the video, and it provides dense, stage-specific rewards. By constraining relations instead of exact poses, the generated reset states naturally encompass variations in object poses and grasps. The dense rewards ensure that RL exploration remains efficient and guided, preventing it from getting lost in high-dimensional state spaces. The entire training process is conducted in simulation, with the learned policy then transferred zero-shot to a real multi-fingered robotic hand.

Notably, it achieves a 6.5% improvement over baseline methods in scenarios with configurations present in the training data. This suggests broad applicability in domains requiring dexterous manipulation where diverse and unpredictable object interactions are common.

**Summary**
Dex-One2Many presents a compelling real-to-sim-to-real framework for learning generalizable dexterous manipulation policies from single human videos. By leveraging sequential scene graphs to guide RL exploration and reward shaping, the method effectively balances data efficiency with broad generalization. The framework's ability to generate diverse reset states and provide dense rewards allows for efficient learning in simulation, which then transfers effectively to real-world robotic systems.
