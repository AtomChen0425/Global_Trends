This analysis examines the safety implications of coding agents in robot manipulation. The core technical insight is that while language models can generate robot control programs without task-specific training, they inherently prioritize task completion over safety constraints. This leads to frequent collisions with obstacles, even when explicitly forbidden by the prompt. The failure stems from the planning phase, where the safety constraint is not effectively integrated as a priority.

The research identifies two key areas where this planning failure manifests: route planning and contact execution. During the routing phase, the agent lacks the ability to conceptualize a "clearing route" and fails to replan when an initial path becomes infeasible due to an obstacle. Similarly, during contact-rich manipulation, the agent does not account for the safety constraint when determining contact points.

To address these limitations, the authors introduce "SafeHarness," a system that incorporates two obstacle-aware mechanisms. The first, obstacle-aware route planning, grounds obstacles as bounding boxes and generates candidate routes as waypoint sequences. This allows the agent to proactively plan, verify, and replan routes before execution, ensuring obstacle avoidance. The second, obstacle-aware contact execution, guides the selection of contact positions to circumvent obstacles.

SafeHarness demonstrates significant improvements in both task success (71.9%) and collision avoidance (87.5%), outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods and the baseline agent without harnesses by substantial margins. This highlights the critical need for explicit safety integration in coding agent frameworks for robust and reliable robot manipulation.
