Its core purpose is to make LLMs accessible without compromising on model size or performance, by overcoming the typical VRAM limitations of GPUs. The core challenge lies in the significant viewpoint variations between aerial and ground perspectives, leading to substantial occlusion and geometric distortions. Traditional 2D image-based methods struggle because their learned features are inherently tied to specific viewpoints, hindering robust cross-view matching.

VR3D addresses this by shifting the paradigm to a unified 3D coordinate space. The key technical innovation is the "View-Robust 3D Representation Interaction" (VR3I) module. The proposed VR3D framework demonstrates significant improvements in aerial-ground person re-identification. Its ability to learn view-independent features by leveraging 3D geometry and semantics makes it highly effective across diverse datasets like CARGO, AG-ReID.v1, and AG-ReID.v2. Coverage measures the thoroughness of a caption in describing factual content, while Precision assesses the factual correctness rate of the caption's assertions. This decoupled evaluation framework provides actionable insights for selecting and optimizing captioning models based on specific downstream application requirements.

**Summary**

CAPEval offers a significant advancement in caption evaluation by disentangling Coverage and Precision. **Background**

Current automated pain assessment systems face significant limitations in real-world clinical settings. These challenges stem from a lack of extensive, clinically validated facial video datasets with precise, sample-level labels. Existing datasets often rely on weaker, sequence-level self-reports, which lack the granularity needed for accurate, automated analysis. Furthermore, while RGB facial cues can be indicative of pain, they are often subtle or nearly neutral, making them difficult to interpret reliably. Although thermal and depth imaging offer richer pain signals, their practical implementation in routine clinical practice is hindered by cost and complexity.

**Technical Implementation**

To overcome these obstacles, the ReMiX-MAE framework is proposed. This is a self-supervised, multimodal masked pretraining approach designed to learn robust facial representations from synchronized RGB, thermal, and depth video data. A key innovation of ReMiX-MAE is its explicit training for robustness to missing modalities. This allows the model to function effectively even when only RGB data is available for deployment, a scenario far more practical in clinical environments. The framework leverages a masked autoencoder architecture, trained to reconstruct missing data channels, thereby learning cross-modal dependencies and generating transferable features.

**Application Scenarios**

The efficacy of ReMiX-MAE is demonstrated through its application to the newly collected Sympathetic Mediated Pain (SMP) dataset. This dataset is specifically designed to address the scarcity of clinically grounded facial pain data, featuring paired pre- and post-treatment recordings across multiple patient visits. Under an RGB-only deployment scenario, ReMiX-MAE shows superior performance compared to an RGB-only masked autoencoder baseline. The framework achieves further performance improvements by generating "pseudo-multimodal" features decoded from RGB, particularly in a more challenging five-class pain intensity classification task. Importantly, ReMiX-MAE exhibits more robust and label-efficient transfer learning capabilities on external datasets, underscoring its value in data-scarce clinical environments.

**Summary**

ReMiX-MAE presents a significant advancement in automated pain assessment by developing a multimodal pretraining framework that learns robust facial representations from synchronized RGB, thermal, and depth data. Its ability to train for missing modality robustness enables practical RGB-only deployment.
