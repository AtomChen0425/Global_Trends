This article addresses the challenge of achieving reliable 3D environmental understanding for autonomous driving, specifically focusing on depth estimation from multi-view surround camera systems. The core problem identified is the limited overlap between adjacent camera views, forcing reliance on monocular appearance cues for depth inference. These monocular cues are susceptible to variations across different cameras, leading to inconsistencies in depth estimation.

The proposed technical solution tackles two primary sources of this cross-image inconsistency. Firstly, it introduces "camera-aware ray embeddings" that condition features on a per-pixel basis. This allows the network to explicitly account for variations in camera intrinsics, ensuring that monocular cues are interpreted consistently regardless of the specific camera capturing them. Secondly, to overcome the limited receptive field of individual images, the method employs cross-image attention. This attention mechanism extends a pixel's context by incorporating information from other images, but crucially, it is constrained to geometrically plausible regions. This geometric constraint is derived from the calibrated rig setup, preventing the model from attending to irrelevant or physically impossible areas. The entire system is trained in a fully self-supervised manner, leveraging photometric consistency as the supervisory signal.

The practical application of this method lies in enhancing the robustness and accuracy of depth estimation for autonomous driving systems. By improving cross-image depth consistency, it directly contributes to more reliable perception pipelines. The evaluations on the DDAD and nuScenes datasets demonstrate significant improvements in both overall depth accuracy and consistency, outperforming existing self-supervised approaches. This suggests the method is effective in real-world scenarios and generalizes well, even to different domains.

In summary, this work presents a novel self-supervised approach to multi-view depth estimation for autonomous driving. By explicitly modeling camera intrinsic variations and intelligently incorporating cross-image context through geometrically constrained attention, the method achieves superior depth accuracy and consistency. This advancement is critical for building more robust and dependable perception systems in autonomous vehicles.
