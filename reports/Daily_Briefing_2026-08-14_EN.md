**Background**

Current video generation approaches often leverage autoencoders to create compact latent spaces for generative models. **Background**

Interactive world models face a fundamental challenge: balancing persistent memory, responsive interaction, and long-horizon generation. Traditional approaches struggle with these conflicting demands. Storing history within the denoiser context or key-value cache leads to escalating costs, forcing a compromise between session length and the depth of retained memory. Furthermore, achieving low-latency interaction typically requires few-step generation, which is inherently limited by the capabilities of its "teacher" model.

**Technical Implementation**

Evoke tackles these limitations by introducing a novel architecture that externalizes persistent world state and re-engineers the teacher model for long-horizon interactive generation. Scene geometry is managed in an external, camera-indexed world state bank. This design ensures that only view-relevant information is retrieved, effectively bounding the denoiser's context regardless of session length. The teacher model is reimagined not as a static generator, but as a tool for long-horizon supervision. Its sparse attention mechanism incorporates chunk-wise grouping, retrieval of distant frames, and a linear-attention global state. This combination results in linear growth of memory and compute requirements, enabling supervision over extended periods. This supervision effectively exposes content drift that might otherwise appear locally plausible. Per-chunk conditioning allows for dynamic prompt changes and event control throughout the generation process. A 30-second distribution-matching objective, employed under self-forced rollouts, transfers these capabilities to a three-step student model. Notably, this student model operates without classifier-free guidance, enhancing its resilience to long-term drift while maintaining responsive conditioning.

**Application Scenarios**

Evoke's architecture, featuring bounded context and recurrent external memory, is designed for open-ended, continuously evolving generation. This makes it suitable for applications requiring dynamic and persistent scene understanding. For instance, in a real-time simulation environment, Evoke could maintain a consistent and evolving world state, allowing for complex interactions and long-term narrative development. The efficiency of the model is demonstrated by its generation speed: on a single H200 GPU at $384\times 640$ resolution, each $1.5\,\mathrm{s}$ chunk is generated in $2.11\,\mathrm{s}$. As a three-step world model, Evoke achieves state-of-the-art performance on WBench and remains competitive on benchmarks like VBench-Long and VBench-2.0, indicating its strong generative capabilities and long-term coherence.

**Summary**

Evoke represents a significant advancement in interactive world modeling by decoupling persistent state from the generation process. Its innovative use of an external world state bank and a redesigned, long-horizon supervising teacher model addresses key limitations of prior methods. This approach enables both efficient, low-latency interaction and robust, long-term coherence in generated content, paving the way for more sophisticated and engaging interactive AI systems.
